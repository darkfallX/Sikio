# Sikio competitive review | November 25, 2026

**Narrow the scope.** Give one producer a corrected subtitle they can publish and a receipt for the reviewer who made that correction. One phrase connects a buyer, a human decision, a file and a payment. That is a reason to keep building. The largest reason to lose is that a judge currently needs two separate demonstrations to believe the sentence “the producer paid for this correction.”

The local original brief, `SIKIO-CONCEPT.md`, already chose one clip, one editor, one payout and one corrected export as the solo cut line. The remaining work is to prove that promise. The builder confirmed full-time availability. This plan uses the brief’s December 10 submission target. A fluent reviewer, a consented fresh recording and access to a funded signing wallet remain unconfirmed.

## Independent assessments

The three reviewers received the same project evidence and formed their verdicts independently. Earlier project rankings were excluded from their assessments.

- **User advocate.** The most plausible first customer is a producer with a trusted Lagos reviewer and a disputed client name. Rev already highlights uncertain words and plays audio from a selected word. Happy Scribe supports human proofreading, sharing and subtitle export. The switching test is whether Sikio saves coordination and combined labor at the quality the producer accepts. The reviewer’s willingness to work for the proposed fee is a separate, unanswered question. Research on African speech recognition supports the existence of errors. It does not measure this customer’s costs. [Rev editor](https://support.rev.com/hc/en-us/articles/29824992702989-Transcription-Editor) · [Happy Scribe subtitles](https://help.happyscribe.com/en/articles/8541414-tutorial-how-to-create-subtitles) · [AfriSpeech-MultiBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14255)
- **Skeptical judge.** The memorable moment is hearing a disputed name, seeing its spelling change at the right second, then seeing the reviewer’s wallet receive the agreed fee. Lugha’s public challenge repository already demonstrates the competitive overlap in paid African-language work. It documents a mobile contributor flow, testnet payout, demo video and downloadable training dataset. The judge verified a successful release transaction. Its final entry status and complete flow were not checked. A team with an observable buyer and a reusable, paid-for output could beat a perfectly functioning Sikio on utility. [Lugha’s project](https://github.com/0xSylla/lugha-hackathon-project/tree/5e0e2864) · [Release transaction](https://explorer-test.qday.info/tx/0xa154cc3fbff881011099d789ccf680adbf3bb26eca3324285f797b3dda108338)
- **Implementation engineer.** Keep the contract design, reviewer UI and subtitle merge. The browser creates an offer in the local ledger, then submits the answer to a local API. The historical CLI makes its own offer, signs a scripted answer and pays against that task. The live transcription verifier checks transaction success without matching the expected payment event and request. The contract also allows the agency or approver to pay an offered task before an editor submits. The web API’s early-accept check applies to that API. ## Changes proposed for approval

1. **Connect one current task to its payment and export.** Budget 3–5 focused days plus one day of rehearsal, conditional on a funded test wallet. Feed actual job and task IDs into the existing operator script. Reserve the agreed fee, relay the editor’s signed correction, and accept it using an authorized agency account. Use one inference request ID across challenge and settlement. Match confirmed escrow and token events to the expected IDs, accounts, fee and correction before marking the task paid. Enforce submission before payment in the contract, test that rule, and verify the new deployment’s source. Keep the old addresses and historical proofs identifiable. A judge should match one exported cue, one correction and one editor balance change, then repeat the operation and see the balance stay put. This buys proof of integration and safety at the cost of a second deployment and a tightly scoped operator-assisted demo.
2. **Test the switch with one producer and their reviewer.** Start recruiting immediately. Allow 1–2 working days once participants and consented clips are available. Use two matched pairs of unfamiliar passages, swap which workflow gets which passage, and have the producer set the acceptance standard first. Record producer and reviewer active time, turnaround, accepted fee, final text and critical names missed against a reference. Compare with the producer’s usual tools and payment cadence. Publish the raw observations with consent, including losses. Until then, label the existing pilot numbers as unverified examples. This provides evidence about one customer and sacrifices the broader savings headline.
3. **Make the supported input promise true.** Allow about 1–2 days. Scope the judged path to a consented, sliceable PCM WAV. Use a real timed model transcript with its origin and actual flagging rule visible, or let a producer nominate a timed phrase from their existing transcript. Report missing transcription explicitly and fail closed if an excerpt cannot be sliced. For private client media, the reviewer must also lack access to the source file and arbitrary ranges. Use public or expressly consented demo material until that boundary is verified. Pass when a second, unfamiliar WAV produces words traceable to its own source, an unavailable model produces a recoverable state, and unsupported input never delivers full audio to Ear. The tradeoff is narrower format and language coverage.

**If only one change fits, do the first.** Keep the existing visual design, Ear, approval, escrow and SRT/VTT export. Defer a marketplace, additional dialects, new wallets and another redesign. Run the cheapest disconfirmation before writing the integration. Have one producer and reviewer resolve two comparable disputed names using their normal handoff and the existing prototype. If they would decline the fee, expend more combined effort, or miss a client-critical name, drop the savings claim. Narrow further to producer-selected disputes if those users value the auditable settlement. If they also see no value there, submit the demonstrator and redirect the remaining product-development time.
